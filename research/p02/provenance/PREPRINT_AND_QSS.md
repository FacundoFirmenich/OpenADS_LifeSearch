# Editorial route for author review

1. First choice for journal submission: Quantitative Science Studies.
2. Requested order: preprint first, journal second. bioRxiv is the preferred
   preprint venue, not an approved posting. bioRxiv's scope permits work from
   other fields if directly relevant to the life sciences and lists
   Scientific Communication and Education. Its screening still evaluates
   scope. The new observation-to-literature audit targets what a researcher
   can find about environments relevant to the search for life; it does not
   measure habitability or detect life. Check fit without recasting it as
   experimental biology.
   If screened out, the author can select a bibliometrics-compatible preprint
   venue; arXiv cs.DL is a possible alternative, used by the close comparator
   Leibel and Bornmann (arXiv:2506.03726, published in QSS).
3. Before upload, confirm author consent, affiliations, disclosures,
   data availability, redistribution rights, and the current QSS
   submission/preprint policy. The official QSS submission page returned
   403 during the check; the comparator establishes precedent, not the
   exact current policy. Do not upload the private raw-data dossier as-is.

## Unsent scope inquiry

Subject: Scope inquiry: how published evidence for planetary habitability is represented in an astrobiology literature archive

Dear bioRxiv editorial team,

We would appreciate a scope check for an original study of how an archived
NASA ADS literature retrieval represents the observations used to assess
environments relevant to the search for life. Across six Solar System
targets, we audit 15 primary studies linking identifiable observations to
local conditions such as solvent, chemical nutrients, and energy, or to
sample access. The archive misses the Cassini south-polar imaging study of
Enceladus and an independent Europa water-vapour study. We also show that
literature trajectory reversals stable to specification choices need not
receive simultaneous statistical support, while several directions are
stable across both estimators and paper-resampling regimes. We do not estimate planetary
habitability or claim life detection. Is this a fit for Scientific
Communication and Education?

Kind regards,
Facundo Firmenich

## Unsent journal cover note

Dear Editors of Quantitative Science Studies,

We offer an empirical study of the distinction between local robustness to
analysis choices and conditional sampling support in publication rankings.
Using six Solar System target literatures, we locate eight trajectory
reversals unanimous within relevant specifications. A shared-bibcode joint
analysis does not certify any of those as a two-direction statistical
reversal, but supports 16 local directions. All 16 retain their unanimous
observed sign after omitting any one year in their window; in the shortest
window only nine of 19 descriptive directions do. A complete map distinguishes
descriptive unanimity from conditional support. The physical-evidence panel
documents two concrete omissions without claiming recall. The paper contributes a
pair- and window-specific interpretation of bibliometric stability that
complements existing work on multiverse inference.

made in preparing this dossier.
